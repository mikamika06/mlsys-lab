Engineers on the fine-tuning team reported anomalous behavior when scaling gradient accumulation steps from 1 to K in our low-level training pipeline. When fine-tuning a model with micro-batch size M and gradient accumulation factor K = 4, the observed loss trajectory and model parameter updates per optimizer step were mathematically identical to running with micro-batch size M and K = 1. This occurs despite processing four times as many total micro-batches per optimizer step and incurring full compute overhead.

Further telemetry shows no explicit Python exceptions, runtime warnings, or invalid floating-point values during training. However, the norm of the accumulated step gradients immediately prior to `optimizer.step()` remains unexpectedly small, matching the magnitude of a single micro-batch pass rather than an accumulated aggregate across K micro-batches.

Your task is to inspect the accumulation pipeline in `gradaccum/accumulator.py` and the diagnostic telemetry in `gradaccum/tracer.py`. Implement the correct gradient accumulation workflow so gradients accumulate properly across micro-batches, quantify the telemetry discrepancy between valid accumulation and silent gradient clearing, and write a regression test in `tests/test_regression.py` that fails whenever gradients are wiped per micro-batch.
