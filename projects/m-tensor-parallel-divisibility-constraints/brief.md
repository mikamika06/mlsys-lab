We recently upgraded our deployment cluster to run our large language models on 8-GPU instances instead of our previous 4-GPU setup. To utilize the new hardware, we increased our Tensor Parallelism degree (tp_size=8 now, instead of tp_size=4). We also quantize our weights to 4-bit, expecting the Marlin kernel to give us a massive throughput boost as it always has.

However, our metrics dashboards are showing a severe regression. The throughput on the 8-GPU setup is actually worse than the 4-GPU setup. When I looked at the torch profiler traces, I noticed that on 8 GPUs, the model is falling back to a slow dequantize-then-gemm kernel for almost all of the `RowParallelLinear` and `ColumnParallelLinear` layers. Marlin is nowhere to be seen, even though the base model config hasn't changed at all!

I suspect this has to do with how the weights are sliced across the GPUs. Marlin has strict shape requirements, and slicing by 8 might be producing local shards that violate them. We need a way to detect when a layer shard becomes ineligible for Marlin, quantify the performance hit analytically, and automatically pad the model's global dimensions minimally during loading so that every shard remains eligible for the fast path regardless of the tensor parallel size.
