Speculative decoding relies on a smaller draft model to propose candidate tokens, which are then verified in parallel by a larger target model. However, in many practical deployment scenarios, the only available or suitable small model has been trained with a completely different tokenizer and vocabulary than the target model.

When tokenizers differ, token IDs cannot be directly mapped or compared between the draft and target models. A naive approach of mapping tokens by ID or directly concatenating decoded strings without precise byte-level alignment results in corrupted context windows, invalid prefix lengths, and degraded sampling distributions. This leads to broken generation pipelines, high rejection rates, or subtle semantic shifts in the output text.

Your task is to build a robust cross-tokenizer speculative decoding module that correctly aligns token sequences at the byte level, maps candidate tokens across disparate vocabularies, verifies target distribution consistency, measures acceptance rates accurately, achieves measurable generation speedups without altering the output distribution, and gracefully handles unknown or unmappable characters during translation.
