We are investigating a low-level memory efficiency anomaly inside our quantization pipeline involving bitsandbytes blockwise-int8 operations. During a routine benchmark pass of our inference kernel, we noticed that applying `bitsandbytes` dynamic quantization directly to real weight tensors results in unexpected floating-point deviations and performance degradation on specific layers, particularly when varying the blocksize parameter between 32, 64, and 128 elements.

Engineers have reported that certain weight columns exhibit severe outlier values that bypass standard aggressive quantization and instead route through the fallback path to maintain numerical stability in fp16. However, our current automated integration tests fail to track whether these outlier columns are correctly detected, routed, and whether the dequantized output matches the expected tensor fidelity under different block sizes.

Your task is to design and implement a robust verification module for bitsandbytes-style blockwise quantization, ensuring block sizes (32, 64, 128) are handled correctly, outlier detection and fallback routing are accurately mapped, and dequantized outputs meet the required mean squared error (MSE) gating threshold against the original unquantized real layers. You must also write a regression test suite that catches any faulty fallback routing logic or incorrect block size indexing.
