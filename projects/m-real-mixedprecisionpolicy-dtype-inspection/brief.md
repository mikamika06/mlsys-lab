# Ticket: Distributed Training Performance and Precision Anomalies

During our recent distributed large language model training runs utilizing fully sharded data parallel frameworks, engineering teams observed severe discrepancies between expected execution efficiency and actual cluster performance. Specifically, cluster telemetry indicates that communication overheads during multi-step training loops do not scale down proportionally when gradient accumulation with deferred synchronization primitives is enabled. Jobs configured to skip gradient synchronization across intermediate micro-steps continue to exhibit communication traffic patterns identical to baseline synchronous training, leading to unexpected interconnect saturation and prolonged iteration times.

Concurrently, validation pipelines running mixed precision configurations report unexpected numerical drift and gradual divergence in loss trajectories when transitioning between standard full-precision accumulation and low-precision representations. Engineers attempting to inspect underlying policy configurations find that policy objects lack robust introspection utilities, making it difficult to verify whether parameter dtypes, reduction dtypes, and buffer dtypes are correctly plumbed through the underlying runtime layers. Furthermore, simple accumulation benchmarks simulating low-precision arithmetic exhibit anomalous rounding behaviors that fail to match theoretical error growth bounds.

Your task is to implement the introspection utilities, communication counters, and numerical accumulation simulators required to accurately diagnose and verify mixed precision policies, deferred synchronization semantics, and precision-specific accumulation error growth within our distributed training toolkit.
