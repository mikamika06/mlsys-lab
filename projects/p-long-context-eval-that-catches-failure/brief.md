# Ticket: 128k context model loses facts in the middle

We are encountering a critical reliability issue with our deployed 128k context window language model. While benchmark scores and marketing specifications claim full support for long documents up to 128,000 tokens, production telemetry and internal spot checks reveal that the model consistently fails to retrieve specific factual information when it is placed in the middle portion of the context window.

When facts or key constraints are located near the absolute beginning or the very end of the prompt, accuracy remains high. However, once the needle is inserted between 25% and 75% of the total prompt length, extraction accuracy drops precipitously, often behaving worse than a much smaller baseline model. Standard evaluation suites—such as short-form QA benchmarks, standard academic leaderboards, and synthetic evaluations that test uniform distribution or simple retrieval—fail to expose this degradation because they do not systematically sweep across positional depths or isolate attention mechanics from tokenization artifacts.

We need a dedicated, automated evaluation and diagnostic pipeline specifically engineered to probe positional robustness across extended contexts. This tooling must programmatically construct needle-in-a-haystack tasks across granular positional strata, compute exact retrieval success curves, disentangle true attention decay from tokenization boundary issues, compare behavior across different context extension mechanisms (such as NTK-aware scaling versus linear interpolation), and reliably reproduce these failure modes so that regressions can be caught automatically during CI/CD before models are pushed to production.
