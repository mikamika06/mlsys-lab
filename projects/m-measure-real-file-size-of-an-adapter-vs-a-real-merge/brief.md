When deploying fine-tuned language models in production environments, engineering teams frequently encounter unexpected storage bloat and performance degradation when transitioning from modular low-rank adaptation weights to fully merged checkpoints. Specifically, measuring the exact on-disk footprint of decoupled adapter weights versus a unified merged model reveals serialization overheads and metadata inflation that do not scale linearly with parameter counts. Furthermore, downstream evaluation uncovers subtle perplexity regressions when a model is merged in floating-point precision and subsequently subjected to low-bit quantization, such as 4-bit GGUF or AWQ formats, compared to maintaining separate adapter structures. Compounding these deployment challenges, automated conversion and exporting pipelines occasionally fail to thoroughly purge all adapter-specific parameter tensors (such as rank-decomposition matrices, scaling coefficients, or dropout states) during the `merge_and_unload` procedure. Residual LoRA keys lingering in the state dictionary trigger silent runtime failures, shape mismatches during inference engine initialization, or incorrect forward passes where adapter branches are inadvertently executed twice. A robust, automated verification suite is urgently required to accurately profile on-disk storage ratios, quantify the compounding precision loss of post-merge quantization, and cryptographically guarantee that all adapter artifacts are fully collapsed into base weights before production packaging.
