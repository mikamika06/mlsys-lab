During peak traffic hours in our prefix-cached inference serving cluster, we observed an erratic and severe degradation in request processing efficiency. Specifically, when serving multiple concurrent streams sharing a large 4k system prompt alongside diverse user suffixes, the overall prefix cache hit rate experiences sudden, catastrophic collapses. Instead of maintaining a steady high reuse ratio, the system repeatedly evicts critical shared prefixes, leading to massive spikes in Time to First Token (TTFT) and severe tail latency inflation. Operators noted that even when the aggregate token budget in the cache pool appears sufficient to hold the shared prefixes and active contexts, specific request sequencing patterns trigger premature evictions. Furthermore, comparative benchmarks measuring the Automatic Prefix Caching (APC) effectiveness show unexpected anomalies where turning APC on versus off yields erratic TTFT ratios under 4k shared system prompts, defying our expected caching gains. We need an investigation and optimization suite to reorder incoming request batches to maximize prefix cache reuse under strict cache capacity constraints, accurately pinpoint the exact evicting request causing hit rate collapse, and rigorously evaluate APC on/off TTFT ratios under heavy shared prompt loads.
