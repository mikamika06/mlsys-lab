We are facing a critical discrepancy between our local simulation environment and the target edge hardware deployment. When running model inference benchmarks inside our simulator, we observe stable, highly optimistic latency numbers. However, when deploying the exact same compiled artifact onto the physical device, the reported latency numbers diverge by a factor of two. Furthermore, successive runs on the same device exhibit an unacceptable variance of up to 30%, making performance regression testing virtually impossible and blocking our production release sign-off.

We suspect that naive benchmarking scripts fail to account for hardware-level dynamics such as thermal throttling, frequency scaling, initialization overheads, and memory allocation spikes during the initial execution phase. Without a rigorous, statistically sound measurement methodology that properly separates cold starts, isolates steady-state performance under thermal equilibrium, and computes required sample sizes with confidence intervals across multiple independent sessions, we cannot trust our telemetry. Your task is to implement a robust on-device latency profiling framework that yields repeatable, manager-ready performance metrics.
