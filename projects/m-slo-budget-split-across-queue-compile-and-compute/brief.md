In high-throughput LLM serving deployments, operators frequently observe intermittent tail latency spikes and unexpected SLO violations during bursty traffic periods. Even when average GPU compute utilization appears healthy and stable, individual requests miss their end-to-end deadlines. Production telemetry indicates that these delays stem from uncontrolled queueing queues backing up before batching, unoptimized kernel compilation pauses kicking in mid-inference when dynamic shapes change, and improper partitioning of the strict time budget between request waiting time, JIT compilation overhead, and token generation compute phases. Without a clear programmatic breakdown and allocation model for these distinct latency components, autoscalers and admission controllers cannot accurately predict or guarantee tail latency bounds, leading to dropped requests and SLA penalties. This project requires implementing a precise SLO budget split utility and optimal batch sizing model to accurately decompose and constrain queue, compile, and compute latencies under strict runtime constraints.
