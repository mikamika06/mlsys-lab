During deep learning training runs, engineers frequently observe intermittent GPU starvation and suboptimal hardware efficiency. Profiling logs and system telemetry indicate that execution stalls occur because compute units occasionally sit idle waiting for incoming batches from the host pipeline. Although hardware resources are powerful, timeline traces reveal mysterious gaps where neither compute nor data transfer is active, pointing to upstream bottlenecks in Python-side data loading workers or inefficient synchronization between host and device memory operations. Tuning batch sizes or blindly adjusting parallel worker counts often yields unpredictable performance degradation rather than stabilization, because teams lack a principled way to measure the exact dataloader wait fraction from raw profiling traces, model the effects of double-buffered host-to-device prefetching overlap, and mathematically determine the minimum number of worker processes required to completely saturate the GPU. Your task is to implement a comprehensive analysis and simulation suite in the pipeline package to diagnose trace wait fractions, simulate double-buffered prefetchers, and calculate worker saturation thresholds accurately.
