During peak traffic hours, our vLLM serving instance exhibits erratic tail latency spikes (p99) that scale non-linearly with request arrival rates. Operators observe that GPU compute utilization remains steady, yet request throughput severely degrades and downstream applications experience frequent timeouts. Investigation suggests that high concurrency leads to recurrent KV cache eviction and request preemption cycles, but we currently lack a systematic tool to ingest server performance logs, extract internal preemption counters, and quantify their statistical correlation with tail latency degradation. Without this analytical capability, engineers cannot reliably determine optimal concurrency thresholds or evaluate the effectiveness of newly proposed preemption and scheduling policies under over-capacity traffic loads. We need a modular analysis toolkit to process simulated server execution traces, compute robust p99 latencies alongside preemption totals, and measure their direct statistical correlation.
