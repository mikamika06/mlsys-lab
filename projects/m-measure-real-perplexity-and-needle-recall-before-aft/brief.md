Engineers notice that after applying a naive context window extension via configuration changes or positional interpolation without fine-tuning, models exhibit unexpected degradation on long-range retrieval tasks and language modeling metrics. Specifically, when evaluating standard evaluation suites or custom needle-in-a-haystack tasks beyond the original pre-training length, such as jumping from two thousand to eight thousand tokens, the model either completely loses the ability to recall specific injected keys or suffers from massive perplexity spikes, rendering the extended context unusable in production workloads. While various training-free scaling heuristics such as linear interpolation, dynamic NTK scaling, or modified base frequencies are widely discussed in literature, teams lack a rigorous, automated way to baseline and track real perplexity changes alongside downstream retrieval recall metrics before and after applying these modifications. Without a dependable measurement pipeline that isolates positional scaling effects on long sequences, it is impossible to verify whether a given modification actually preserves foundational language understanding or merely introduces silent hallucinations and token degradation.
