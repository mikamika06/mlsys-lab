We are observing silent precision drift and layout mismatches when serializing and deserializing quantized large language model weights in the GGUF format, specifically under the complex K-quant superblocks used by llama.cpp. In our low-level inference pipeline, when working with the `Q4_K` quantization scheme, weights are grouped into 256-element superblocks containing sub-blocks, scales, and mins packed into exact bitfields.

Currently, our round-trip quantization pipeline fails to match the reference byte stream precisely. When we encode float weights into the `Q4_K` layout and then unpack/decode them back, or vice versa, the serialized byte representation drifts from the exact bit-for-bit layout expected by standard llama.cpp readers. This byte-exact divergence leads to subtle checksum mismatches and verification errors when loading pre-quantized weights or cross-validating with upstream GGUF tools. Furthermore, engineers cannot pinpoint which specific sub-blocks inside a 256-element superblock contribute the highest mean squared error (MSE) relative to the unquantized weights, making it difficult to optimize error distribution. Lastly, our comparative analysis between `Q4_K` and `Q4_0` formats at matched bits-per-weight (bpw) lacks empirical validation, leaving us uncertain about the precise quantization error trade-offs across different tensor shapes.

Your task is to implement a robust low-level module that achieves byte-exact round-trip Q4_K quantization, pinpoints high-error sub-blocks within superblocks, and quantifies error differences against Q4_0 at matched bits per weight.
