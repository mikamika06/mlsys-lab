We've been scaling our Megatron-LM-style transformer training to significantly larger context lengths, and we are abruptly hitting Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors during the forward pass. Our parameters are sharded correctly across 8 A100 GPUs using Tensor Parallelism (TP), and they fit comfortably in HBM. 

When we profiled the memory allocations, we noticed massive spikes happening just outside the TP blocks—specifically at the boundaries where the LayerNorm and Dropout operations occur. It appears that every single GPU is holding an exact, fully replicated copy of these boundary activation tensors (which have the shape `seq_len * batch_size * hidden_size`). As we scale `seq_len` up, these redundant copies blow up our memory, essentially defeating the purpose of scaling our TP dimension.

Your task is to implement Megatron Sequence Parallelism (SP) in our TP regions. SP shards the sequence dimension across the TP group for these non-matmul operations. In M1, rigorously check the theoretical memory savings and communication volumes to ensure SP doesn't increase our network overhead. In M2, build a toy implementation of the forward pass using `All-Gather` and `Reduce-Scatter` primitives to prove structural correctness. Finally, ensure the core invariant—that SP reduces memory compared to standard TP—is caught by regression tests in M3.
