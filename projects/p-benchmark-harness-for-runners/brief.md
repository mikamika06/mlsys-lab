Engineers measuring local LLM inference models across different development workstations and environments frequently encounter wildly inconsistent throughput numbers, with results such as 22, 31, and 44 tokens per second under identical nominal workloads. These discrepancies lead to contentious code reviews, false alarms regarding performance regressions in continuous integration pipelines, and an inability to reliably verify whether a system optimization genuinely improved inference speed or if the observed variation was merely random background noise.

The project requires building a robust, standardized benchmarking harness that eliminates variance and establishes trust in measurement results. The harness must systematically enforce proper warm-up procedures, hardware synchronization, statistical robustness using medians and interquartile ranges, explicit separation of prefill and decode phases, thermal degradation accounting on laptops, confidence-interval sample size estimation, and multi-run consistency checks that output machine-readable reports for automated CI integration.
