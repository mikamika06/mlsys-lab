Our large language model training runs have become increasingly erratic during multi-node scale-out experiments, presenting a severe and perplexing operational bottleneck. Specifically, engineers on the infrastructure team report that prolonged training jobs periodically exhibit sudden loss spikes followed by irreversible numerical divergence, crashing with NaN gradients during deep transformer backward passes. When attempting to reproduce these exact failure modes across different hardware nodes or subsequent job resumptions using identical initialization seeds and hyperparameter configurations, the training trajectories diverge unpredictably within a few hundred iterations.

To debug this non-reproducibility, teams enabled the framework's strict deterministic execution mode for attention operators and gradient reductions. While this successfully eliminated non-reproducible divergence across job restarts, it immediately introduced an unexpected performance regression. Training throughput dropped precipitously, GPU utilization plummeted, and peak memory consumption fluctuated wildly when combining strict determinism with selective activation checkpointing strategies in FlashAttention layers.

Your task is to analyze, measure, and optimize the overheads associated with deterministic backward passes, diagnose the root causes of numerical divergence in non-deterministic accumulation paths, and build a robust modeling utility that balances memory footprint and execution latency under checkpointed attention workflows. You must implement the underlying analysis modules in the `detcost` package and write a rigorous regression test suite that catches faulty cost modeling or broken determinism invariants.
