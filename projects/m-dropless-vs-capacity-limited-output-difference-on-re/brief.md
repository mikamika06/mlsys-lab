We are implementing a low-level Mixture of Experts (MoE) routing engine component in `mlsys-lab` to analyze the exact output difference between dropless execution and capacity-limited execution on real router distributions, and to pick the minimum capacity factor that satisfies a strict token drop-rate budget.

When scaling sparse Mixture of Experts models, hardware memory and compute constraints often require imposing a per-expert capacity limit. Tokens assigned to an expert beyond its token capacity are dropped or bypassed, which saves compute and communication overhead but can degrade model accuracy due to dropped token information. Conversely, dropless execution routes all tokens to their assigned experts regardless of capacity boundaries, leading to dynamic, unpredictable load imbalances and variable execution times.

Our goal in this exercise unit is to analyze the exact output discrepancies under real router probability distributions and to design an automated selector that finds the minimum capacity factor meeting a target drop-rate budget without exceeding error tolerances. Your code will process real token-to-expert router logits and routing probabilities, compute the resulting output differences between dropless and capacity-limited regimes, and implement regression checks to ensure robust behavior under varying expert load profiles.
