We are implementing an LRU prefix-cache simulator to analyze prompt caching efficiency in vLLM-style serving engines. In our current deployment setup, prefix caching allows reusing KV cache blocks across requests with shared prompt prefixes, managed via a Least Recently Used (LRU) eviction policy over block indices. However, our monitoring shows discrepancies between theoretical hit rates and production Prometheus counters, and our cache trace analysis pipeline fails when trying to correctly reproduce eviction sequences under reverse-order free operations or calculate accurate hit ratios from raw token traces.

When running our caching simulation harness against real workloads, the custom eviction sequence verification often breaks because reverse-order deallocations corrupt block tracking state. Furthermore, our offline trace predictor does not accurately model the exact hit rates observed in live Prometheus scraping metrics, leading to miscalculated capacity planning and wasted GPU memory. We need a robust, three-stage low-level simulator module that implements exact LRU prefix caching prediction, correct reverse-order free tracing, and precise Prometheus counter integration for hit rate calculation without relying on external non-deterministic components.
