We are observing elevated memory consumption and severe cache contention in our low-level key-value cache serving infrastructure under long-context multi-turn workloads. During extended inference sessions, the KV cache footprint grows linearly with sequence length, eventually triggering out-of-memory errors or severe tail-latency spikes due to excessive memory pressure and DRAM bandwidth saturation during attention evaluation.

Our current naive caching approach retains every past token's key-value pair indiscriminately across all layers and attention heads. However, empirical analysis of transformer attention dynamics reveals that attention weights are highly concentrated. Specifically, a small subset of initial tokens acts as persistent attention sinks that anchor the attention distribution, while other regions contain heavy-hitter tokens that capture crucial context. Furthermore, modern optimization strategies such as StreamingLLM, H2O (Heavy-Hitter Oracle), and SnapKV demonstrate that we can drastically reduce the active KV cache footprint by intelligently selecting and retaining only the critical token indices—combining attention sinks, heavy-hitter scores from attention matrices, and pooled observation-window scoring—without sacrificing model perplexity or generation quality.
