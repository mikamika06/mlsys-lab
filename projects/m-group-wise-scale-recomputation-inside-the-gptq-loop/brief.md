# Group-wise Scale Recomputation in GPTQ

When running low-bit quantization using the GPTQ algorithm with group-wise weight scaling (such as grouping weights into blocks of 128 elements along rows or columns), engineers frequently observe a distinct degradation in signal-to-noise ratio and increased output error downstream, despite the algorithm properly accumulating inverse Hessian error compensation and updating remaining unquantized weights step by step.

When inspecting intermediate states during the quantization pass, the reconstructed weight matrix diverges significantly from expected reference values in layers configured with fine-grained group-wise quantization scales. Profiling internal tensor computations reveals that while individual weight elements are iteratively modified and quantized during the sequential Cholesky-based update steps, the pre-computed group-wise scaling factors and zero-points remain completely static, relying exclusively on metrics derived from the initial unquantized weight matrix before the optimization loop begins. As subsequent columns are quantized and errors are diffused across the block, the local distribution and magnitude of weights within each group shift substantially, rendering the initial static scales sub-optimal for the newly adjusted values and causing severe numerical drift.

Your objective is to identify and resolve the routine responsible for scale handling during the GPTQ optimization pass to ensure group-wise scales are correctly updated and recomputed as weights evolve throughout the loop, eliminating numerical discrepancies and restoring high-fidelity quantization output.
