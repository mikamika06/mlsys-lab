When benchmarking low-level ML systems, engineers frequently observe erratic performance distributions, especially during the initial capture windows or when running GPU kernels under high memory or compute pressure. In this unit, we investigate systematic measurement distortions arising from heavy-tailed latency profiles, cold-start artifacts, and discrepancies between event-based timers and wall-clock measurements.

You are investigating a benchmarking suite for FlashAttention operations where the recorded execution times exhibit significant tail behavior. Simple summary statistics such as the arithmetic mean fail to capture typical performance because extreme outlier iterations skew the distribution upward, while failing to account for startup overhead contaminates short runs. Furthermore, asynchronous execution models mean that standard wall-clock timing can diverge wildly from actual hardware event intervals if synchronization barriers are missing or misconfigured.

Your task is to implement a robust benchmarking and telemetry analysis pipeline that accurately extracts median latency under heavy-tailed noise, quantifies the exact inflation factor caused by omitting a proper warmup phase, and validates event-to-wall-clock timing agreement across multiple workload configurations. You will build core analysis functions in Python and numpy, ensuring that your test suite rigorously catches faulty benchmarking methodologies.
