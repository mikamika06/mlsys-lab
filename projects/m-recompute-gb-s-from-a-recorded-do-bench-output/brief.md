When profiling custom Triton kernels across multiple block configurations, our benchmarking harness outputs raw execution times and byte counts via `do_bench`. However, engineers on the team are currently unable to reliably establish performance regressions or verify whether our memory-bound operations are actually saturating the hardware bandwidth. Specifically, when we inspect the recorded profiling logs, the throughput figures computed manually often disagree across different workstations, and there is no automated way to determine the theoretical lower-bound execution time dictated by hardware roofline models. Furthermore, attempting to identify the optimal block size configuration by visual inspection of raw bandwidth sweeps has led to inconsistent tuning decisions, with sub-optimal block sizes being selected for production deployments. We urgently need a robust, programmatic pipeline to recompute effective gigabytes per second from recorded benchmarking outputs, derive precise lower-bound roofline execution times, and automatically isolate the hardware saturation knee across varying block sizes without relying on manual guesswork or ad-hoc spreadsheet calculations.
