Engineers deploying cutting-edge multimodal and large language models onto Apple Silicon devices via CoreML are encountering severe performance degradation and unexpectedly high CPU fallback rates during production inference runs. When inspecting execution traces from recent edge deployment passes, telemetry shows that intermediate tensor reshaping stages involving high-dimensional tensors—specifically those exceeding rank 5—frequently cause the Apple Neural Engine (ANE) to abruptly drop residency, forcing subsequent compute kernels to fall back to the CPU or GPU and tanking overall throughput. Furthermore, automated optimization pipelines currently lack a standardized way to evaluate compute-plan JSON logs to quantify actual hardware residency ratios, and there is no programmatic method to compare competing model architectures prior to export to determine which variant will better preserve native ANE execution boundaries. Without structured mechanisms to safely rewrite high-rank shapes, assess compile-time placement scores, and enforce architectural regression invariants, deployment teams are left blind to runtime hardware regressions until physical device testing fails.
