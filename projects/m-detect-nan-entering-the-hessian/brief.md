During large-scale post-training quantization runs on modern transformer architectures, production pipelines occasionally encounter sudden failures where optimization jobs terminate abruptly with linear algebra errors or produce severely degraded weights accompanied by catastrophic perplexity spikes. Engineers investigating core dumps and runtime traces note that certain sensitive linear layers propagate unstable activations that introduce infinite or undefined floating-point values straight into second-order Hessian accumulation routines. This corrupts the curvature estimation matrices, rendering subsequent Cholesky decompositions or eigenvalue inversions invalid. Furthermore, automated calibration runners currently lack an explicit decision heuristic to evaluate whether Hessian-based second-order optimization should be dynamically skipped in favor of a simpler, robust model-free post-training quantization method when numerical conditioning degrades or invalid values appear. Without a programmatic safeguard, automated model compression pipelines either crash or silently output broken artifacts, requiring manual intervention across hundreds of model variants.
