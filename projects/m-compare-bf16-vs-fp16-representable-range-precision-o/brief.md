During execution of large language models on Apple Silicon devices utilizing the Metal framework, engineers have observed sporadic and puzzling numeric corruption within transformer attention blocks and feed-forward networks. Specifically, when handling activation tensors containing large magnitude values or extreme scaling factors, downstream outputs occasionally evaluate to NaN or exhibit severe precision degradation without triggering any explicit hardware exceptions or runtime warnings. This silent numerical failure propagates rapidly through subsequent layers, ultimately causing training loss spikes, gradient explosion, or catastrophic divergence during fine-tuning workflows. Furthermore, comparative analysis of tensor buffers serialized through standard NumPy roundtrips versus native MLX representations reveals unexplained discrepancies in precision and dynamic range handling, particularly near the representational boundaries of half-precision floating-point formats. We require a comprehensive diagnostic and mitigation library that can rigorously analyze float16 versus bfloat16 representable ranges and precision characteristics on extreme-value inputs, accurately quantify serialization roundtrip precision losses, and implement robust reduction safeguards that detect potential overflow conditions and safely upcast computations to higher-precision accumulators to preserve numerical stability across all edge hardware deployments.
