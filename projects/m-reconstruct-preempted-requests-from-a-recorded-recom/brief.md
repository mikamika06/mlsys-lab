We are experiencing intermittent serving stalls and severe latency spikes during peak load in our vLLM deployment cluster. When investigating the cluster nodes under heavy concurrency, operations engineers notice that the serving throughput drops precipitously, and request completion times exhibit a heavy tail distribution with many requests timing out at the client gateway.

Looking through the raw node console output and stdout logs, there are recurrent streams of warning lines indicating that requests are being dynamically preempted or swapped out back to CPU memory or dropped from the running batch to make room for incoming prefill requests, accompanied by messages containing RECOMPUTE tags. However, because the log stream is massive and interleaved across multiple concurrent worker processes and engines, it is nearly impossible for the on-call team to manually trace which specific request IDs were preempted at what timestamps, or how many tokens were involved in these disruption events. Furthermore, we suspect that our current configuration of batch token limits is exacerbating the issue by allowing long-running decode phases to monopolize batch capacity, leading to sustained prefill starvation where new prompts sit in the waiting queue indefinitely without ever entering the scheduler's running batch.

Your task is to build a robust diagnostic library that parses these recorded RECOMPUTE warning logs to accurately reconstruct the exact set of preempted request sequences, and implements an analytical helper to derive the optimal max_num_batched_tokens threshold required to prevent prefill starvation under specified queue pressure conditions. You must also author a regression test suite that catches regressions in the starvation analysis logic.
