We are working on low-level quantization tooling within the GGUF and llama.cpp ecosystem, specifically focusing on quality measurement metrics such as Perplexity (PPL) and Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD).

Recently, when evaluating a new battery of experimental quantization formats ranging from high-precision down to extreme low-bit configurations, our automated ranking pipeline showed unexpected anomalies. While Perplexity gives a direct measure of next-token prediction loss over a calibration text, computing it requires heavy forward passes across large evaluation sets, making quick iterative screening expensive. To optimize our workflow, we often rely on proxy metrics like Kullback-Leibler Divergence against the unquantized baseline logits, which can be computed much faster over smaller sample batches.

However, during our latest sweep across various model layers and block sizes, two major issues surfaced in our evaluation harness:

1. **Monotonicity and Ordering Disagreements**: When sorting our candidate quantizations by their aggregate KLD relative to the unquantized baseline, we noticed that the resulting ranking sometimes violates expected monotonicity against quantization size (in bits-per-weight or file size). Even more critically, a strict inspection revealed instances where a model quantized with a supposedly superior bit-width yields a lower KLD score than a tighter quantization, yet exhibits a worse Perplexity score—indicating a fundamental divergence between our proxy metric and true evaluation loss.
2. **Sample Size Derivation for PPL Differences**: Because PPL estimates are subject to variance depending on the validation text corpus and token length, we lack a principled way to automatically derive the required sample size (number of tokens or evaluation batches) needed to reliably resolve a target statistically significant PPL difference between two competing quantization tiers. Without a robust derivation, our automated tests either under-sample (leading to noisy, flaky gate failures) or over-sample (wasting precious CI compute cycles).

We need a clean, modular Python package that implements these core evaluation mechanics: accurately ranking quantization candidates by KLD, verifying their monotonicity properties, deriving the necessary sample sizes to resolve PPL deltas, and detecting specific PPL/KLD ordering disagreements. Furthermore, we need a robust regression testing suite to ensure that our calculation pipelines maintain their invariants under edge cases.
