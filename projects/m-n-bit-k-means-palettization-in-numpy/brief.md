Low-level model deployment teams at edge compute targets are running into severe memory capacity walls when trying to fit large language models into constrained embedded hardware limits. Specifically, standard quantization methods like uniform per-tensor or per-channel scaling often leave substantial amounts of quantization error, or they require bulky metadata overhead that bloats memory usage.

Engineers have attempted to implement custom codebook-based palettization (k-means quantization) using numpy, but they report two major issues. First, calculating the exact memory footprint of a compressed tensor—including the centroid lookup table, indices, and any padding or bit-packing headers—frequently mismatches hardware expectations, leading to out-of-bounds errors or misaligned pointer access during run-time kernel loading. Second, teams are struggling to evaluate the trade-offs between scalar palettization (where individual scalar weights are mapped to codebook entries) versus vector palettization (where small blocks or vectors of weights share indices) when constrained to an exact equivalent bitrate.

Your task is to build a robust, numpy-based module that handles n-bit k-means palettization cleanly, accurately calculates exact byte consumption down to the bit level, and provides comparative tooling for scalar versus vector palettization strategies under strict bitrate parity constraints. You will implement the core quantization routines, byte calculation utilities, comparison metrics, and a comprehensive test suite to safeguard your logic against structural regressions.
