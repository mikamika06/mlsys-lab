When running Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) workloads across multiple nodes with all-to-all communication for token dispatch and combine, our training pipeline suffers from severe latency spikes and unpredictable bottlenecks. Specifically, at certain sequence lengths and token drop rates, the distributed training jobs stall unexpectedly because the communication fabric saturates, yet scaling up the all-to-all bandwidth does not consistently improve throughput. Furthermore, when placing expert weights across our GPU ranks, certain worker nodes become heavily congested while others sit idle during the token routing phase, causing the overall execution time of the layer to be bottlenecked by the most loaded rank. Engineers report that tuning expert placement manually yields inconsistent performance, and the transition point where compute-bound execution gives way to communication-bound execution remains completely mysterious. We need a systematic mechanism to evaluate the roofline crossover characteristics of our dispatch and combine phases and an optimal expert bin-packing strategy to flatten the load distribution across GPUs without guesswork.
