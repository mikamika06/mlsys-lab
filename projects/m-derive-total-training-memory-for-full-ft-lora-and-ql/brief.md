We are experiencing a memory estimation failure in our multi-tier training workflow when scaling from full fine-tuning to LoRA and QLoRA variants. The engineering team has reported that our current manual memory calculations are either over-estimating or under-estimating peak memory consumption under various hardware constraints, leading to unexpected Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors during job scheduling.

When configuring fine-tuning runs for large language models, engineers need to compute the exact memory footprint required for parameters, gradients, optimizer states, and activations across different methods (full-FT, LoRA, and QLoRA) at a fixed parameter count. Furthermore, as models scale up, the memory ratios between full fine-tuning and LoRA, as well as between LoRA and QLoRA, exhibit distinct scaling trends that our scheduling infrastructure currently fails to capture.

Finally, our deployment pipeline lacks an automated classifier to determine which training method is feasible under restricted hardware budgets (such as single-GPU consumer cards, mid-tier accelerators, and large enterprise clusters). We need a robust computational module that correctly derives training memory, analyzes scaling ratios, and evaluates hardware budget compatibility without hardcoded approximations.
