When deploying large Mixture of Experts (MoE) models using GGUF and llama.cpp on consumer hardware or edge devices, memory capacity becomes a severe bottleneck. The entire model parameters, especially across dozens of expert layers, frequently exceed the available VRAM budget. A naive approach of offloading entire layers or uniform subsets of experts to system RAM drastically degrades inference throughput due to high PCI-e transfer overhead and non-uniform memory access patterns during token generation. System operators observe severe latency spikes and sluggish token generation rates whenever MoE routing hits offloaded weights. However, not all experts are utilized equally during inference; a significant portion of expert parameters exhibit extremely low activation frequencies across diverse typical workloads. Total offloading is overly aggressive and unnecessary, destroying execution speed. The system requires an intelligent, selective offloading mechanism that identifies and targets precisely those expert tensors and components that are rarely activated, keeping hot, frequently accessed experts resident in fast VRAM while maintaining strict memory limits and preserving output fidelity across varying model architectures and workloads.
