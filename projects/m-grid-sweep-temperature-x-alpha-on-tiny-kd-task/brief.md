Our knowledge distillation pipeline is exhibiting strange training dynamics when we attempt to jointly tune the distillation temperature and the mixing coefficient alpha on our tiny proxy task. Specifically, when running automated sweeps across various temperature values and alpha weightings, the resulting student models either suffer from catastrophic gradient vanishing or completely ignore the teacher's soft targets, collapsing back to standard cross-entropy performance. Furthermore, empirical gradient norms measured during the initial training steps deviate significantly from theoretical expectations, suggesting that our implementation fails to properly account for the characteristic scaling factor associated with softmax temperature scaling. Compounding this issue, when teacher logits are subject to minor perturbations or quantization noise, the effective temperature experienced by the student shifts unpredictably, destabilizing convergence and rendering our hyperparameter grids non-transferable across different noise regimes. We need a robust implementation that correctly models the temperature-alpha interaction, numerically verifies the exact gradient scaling behavior, and computes the precise effective-temperature shift under noisy teacher logit conditions.
