Our distributed large language model training job is experiencing severe activation memory blowup and silent accuracy degradation during long-sequence runs. The activation checkpointing layer is currently configured with a naive uniform policy that recomputes every alternating layer, but this ignores the heterogeneous compute-to-memory ratio of individual transformer blocks, leading to massive memory bottlenecks in attention-heavy segments and wasteful redundant forward passes in feed-forward layers.

Furthermore, when training with dropout enabled alongside activation checkpointing, our validation loss curves drift catastrophically and training diverges. Investigation shows that intermediate random number generator states are not correctly preserved or re-seeded during the recomputation pass, causing dropout masks to mismatch between the initial forward pass and the backward recomputation pass. This breaks gradient estimation accuracy and introduces systemic noise into the optimization trajectory.

Compounding these issues, our attempts to integrate PyTorch compile with minimum-cut graph partitioning for automatic recomputation have failed to replicate the fine-grained selective policies we require. The compiler's automated min-cut recomputation cuts graphs at suboptimal tensor boundaries, causing excessive memory retention or redundant tensor reallocations that degrade overall throughput. We need to design a robust, selective checkpointing policy framework that determines optimal checkpoint placement based on memory cost versus compute savings, handles dropout consistency correctly without explicit global RNG preservation hacks, and safely bridges custom selective policies with compilation frameworks.
