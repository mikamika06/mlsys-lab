Our edge export pipeline for low-power neural accelerators is experiencing severe output quality degradation when exporting post-training sparse quantized models. Downstream applications report elevated reconstruction mean squared error (MSE) on weight tensors, and memory budgets on embedded targets are frequently violated during multi-layer optimization passes.

The root cause lies in how our quantization toolchain handles structured vs unstructured pruning alongside low-bit palettization. Specifically, the team lacks standard utilities to accurately evaluate the mean squared error (MSE) penalty of $n:m$ structured block sparsity against unstructured magnitude pruning at equal target sparsity ratios. Because $n:m$ constraints enforce local block structure (such as keeping exactly $n$ out of every $m$ contiguous weights), $n:m$ pruning typically incurs higher weight reconstruction error than global unstructured pruning at the same total sparsity.

Additionally, our current pipeline treats pruning and weight palettization (uniform codebook quantization) as isolated passes rather than balancing a joint prune-then-palettize error budget. When palettizing remaining non-zero weights into a fixed bit-width codebook, the error tradeoff varies depending on the chosen bit-depth and pruning mask. Furthermore, we lack a global optimization mechanism to assign per-layer prune-vs-palettize strategies given a target average bit footprint across a network. We need a unified toolchain module that measures $n:m$ versus unstructured reconstruction MSE at equivalent sparsity, solves joint prune-then-palettize error budgets for individual layers, and performs per-layer strategy assignment under strict global bit constraints.
