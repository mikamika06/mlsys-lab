Our quantization pipeline is seeing a persistent degradation in perplexity on large language model activations when moving from per-tensor static quantization to fine-grained block-wise schemes, and the telemetry indicates our observer selection is suspect. During offline calibration over a calibration corpus of representative tokens, our current calibration strategy blindly applies standard minmax boundary tracking to locate the clipping thresholds and assumes a symmetric zero-point centered distribution for all weight and activation tensors.

However, high-density outliers in intermediate activation layers skew the minmax range drastically, leaving the vast majority of the quantization bins underutilized and inflating the overall quantization noise. Furthermore, forcing a symmetric zero-point configuration when the underlying tensor activation distribution is heavily skewed introduces a systematic non-zero quantization bias that degrades token generation quality. We need to implement an alternative Mean Squared Error (MSE) observer that searches for optimal clipping bounds by minimizing the expected quantization distortion over the calibration batch.

In addition, our scheme parser needs to robustly unpack string descriptors into structured quantization argument objects so that observers can correctly configure their search spaces for symmetric versus asymmetric clipping. Finally, we need a rigorous quantification module to explicitly measure the exact bias introduced by ignoring the true zero-point offset in asymmetric tensors, ensuring our regression test suite catches any regressions in observer clipping optimization and zero-point handling.
