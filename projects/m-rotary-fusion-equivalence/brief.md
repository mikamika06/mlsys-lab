We are observing significant performance regressions and subtle numerical discrepancies during the autoregressive decode phase of our large language model serving pipeline when utilizing optimized flash-attention kernels with integrated rotary position embeddings (RoPE). During prolonged generation sessions, particularly as the KV cache length scales up into tens of thousands of tokens, several anomalies emerge. First, output logits begin to drift beyond acceptable floating-point tolerances when comparing our custom fused rotary kernels against the reference separate-application implementations, even though single-step tests pass. Second, when sweeping over the `num_splits` parameter for parallel reduction in our flash-attention decode path, execution times exhibit erratic spikes and suboptimal performance across varying cache lengths, failing to track the theoretical latency vs cache length curve. Operators report that latency does not scale linearly or predictably with context length, leading to severe tail-latency violations under heavy load. We need a robust implementation that guarantees exact numerical equivalence for fused rotary operations, correctly determines the optimal `num_splits` configuration across cache length regimes, and accurately produces and validates the decode latency versus cache length performance profile without regressions.
