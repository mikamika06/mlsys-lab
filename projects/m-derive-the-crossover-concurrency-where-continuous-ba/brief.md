The serving infrastructure team has encountered persistent performance anomalies when attempting to evaluate and select inference backends for production LLM deployment pipelines. Specifically, comparative reports between continuous batching engines like vLLM and single-request optimized engines like Ollama show conflicting throughput advantages depending on concurrency levels and hardware topologies. Furthermore, published benchmark suites circulating across various internal repositories exhibit severe discrepancies, with raw numbers reported at mismatched precision tiers (such as mixing INT4 quantization with FP16 baselines), omitting essential warm-up iterations, or evaluating under unrealistic single-request loads that fail to reflect multi-tenant production traffic profiles. Consequently, engineers are currently unable to establish a mathematically sound, reproducible baseline for engine selection, leading to suboptimal resource provisioning, unexpected latency spikes under load, and unreliable capacity planning across our GPU clusters. We urgently require a systematic module to derive true throughput crossover points under continuous batching dynamics, normalize disparate external benchmark metrics into consistent tokens-per-second-per-GPU units at equalized precision levels, and implement rigorous automated auditing mechanisms to detect configuration biases, quantization mismatches, and protocol omissions in incoming performance reports.
