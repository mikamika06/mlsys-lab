During large-scale distributed pretraining runs across hundreds of heterogeneous nodes, our training clusters occasionally experience sudden, unrecoverable loss explosions that either crash the model or leave training trapped in invalid NaN states. Engineers report that these spikes often appear completely unprovoked, occurring mid-epoch without any modifications to learning rate schedules, global batch sizes, or data ingestion pipelines. In some diagnostic traces, individual worker ranks diverge silently, producing intermediate gradient tensors that gradually drift apart before triggering a sudden catastrophic collapse across all synchronized GPUs. Furthermore, validation checks reveal that identical model initializations and input batches produce noticeable numerical divergence when executed under different physical GPU topologies or varying node counts, raising serious concerns regarding floating-point reduction-order non-associativity across distributed aggregation trees. While standard gradient clipping helps temporarily, it consistently fails to catch distributed overflow events where a single outlying worker experiences an unmasked Inf or NaN during all-reduce communication phases, leaving other participating ranks entirely out of sync. We require a robust diagnostic and mitigation architecture to quantify non-associativity errors, automatically identify offending ranks or training steps via statistical outlier detection rules during loss spikes, and enforce global synchronization semantics to gracefully skip or recover from distributed overflows before training corruption spreads.
