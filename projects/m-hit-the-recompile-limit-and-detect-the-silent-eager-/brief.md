We are running a distributed multi-GPU training loop in PyTorch with `torch.compile()` enabled across multiple transformer layers handling dynamic sequence lengths and batch sizes. During extensive long-form pretraining jobs spanning several hours, our monitoring infrastructure reports that training throughput abruptly collapses after processing a few hundred thousand iterations. Instead of throwing an explicit compilation error or aborting with a stack trace, execution mysteriously slows down to speeds characteristic of uncompiled eager execution. This performance degradation occurs without any warning messages logged to standard output or standard error, making it extremely difficult to isolate when and why the runtime abandons the optimized compiled graph cache.

Your task is to diagnose the underlying causes of this performance cliff by analyzing compilation trace logs and runtime execution behavior. You need to implement tools that parse compilation events, accurately track unique graph recompilations against cache size limitations, detect the exact moment execution falls back to eager mode without raising exceptions, and write a robust test suite to safeguard against silent fallbacks in production pipelines.
