We are observing severe failures when evaluating long-context large language models on needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) and RULER-style multi-hop benchmarks. Specifically, our current evaluation pipeline exhibits erratic accuracy drops at medium context lengths, and our aggregate retrieval accuracy scores fail to reproduce the classic U-shaped "lost-in-the-middle" curve documented in literature. When running models over contexts spanning from 4k to 64k tokens with varying injection depths (from 0% to 100%), the heatmap visualization tool either throws indexing errors or produces flat, uninformative scoring matrices that hide actual attention degradation. Furthermore, our task generator fails to correctly construct multi-hop aggregation prompts that isolate retrieval failures from reasoning failures, making it impossible to audit whether our models are genuinely losing information or simply failing to aggregate retrieved facts. We need a robust, deterministic evaluation harness and task generator implemented in pure Python and NumPy that correctly builds RULER-style tasks, computes precise retrieval metrics like recall at k across depth-length heatmaps, and includes a comprehensive regression test suite to prevent silent breakages in accuracy computation.
