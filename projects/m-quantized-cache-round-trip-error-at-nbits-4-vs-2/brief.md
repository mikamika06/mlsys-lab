When deploying quantized key-value caches using 4-bit versus 2-bit precision in our high-throughput serving pipeline, our downstream generation logs show unexpected degradation and numerical divergence under high concurrency limits. Specifically, memory footprints match expected static cache bounds during initial allocation, but cross-process deserialization yields silent state corruption or shape mismatches when reloading pre-computed cache files from disk storage. Furthermore, empirical evaluations of round-trip quantization error reveal that lowering precision from 4 bits to 2 bits introduces severe perplexity spikes and out-of-bound decoding anomalies that standard validation suites fail to catch before deployment. Engineers are observing intermittent NaN outputs and incorrect token sequences during peak load testing when switching cache serialization backends. To resolve these operational instabilities, we must implement robust verification of quantization error metrics between 4-bit and 2-bit representations, rigorously calculate correct StaticCache tensor shapes based on maximum batch sizes and sequence lengths, and build a reliable disk serialization and cross-process restoration workflow for pre-allocated caches without losing precision or failing safety invariants.
