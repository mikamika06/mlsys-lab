We are running speculative decoding with an n-gram or prompt-lookup mechanism on our low-latency inference engine, and we have noticed a stark bimodal divergence in performance across different workloads. When serving code generation, repetitive document templating, or long-form copy-heavy inputs, the proposal acceptance rate skyrockets past seventy percent, yielding near-linear speedups. However, when we switch the traffic over to creative writing tasks, chat dialogues, or open-ended instruction following, the n-gram proposal mechanism stalls out, with acceptance rates dropping into the single digits, frequently performing worse than standard autoregressive generation due to overhead.

We need to instrument our n-gram speculative decoding pipeline to properly analyze, measure, and explain this acceptance rate gap between copy-heavy prompts and creative-writing tasks. Right now, our tracking is limited to raw aggregate tokens per second, which obscures why certain workloads fail to leverage prefix matches. We require a dedicated analysis module that extracts n-gram match distributions, computes distinct acceptance rate metrics conditioned on prompt text entropy and repetition indices, and provides automated regression safeguards to ensure our evaluation harness correctly identifies workload sensitivity before deployment.
