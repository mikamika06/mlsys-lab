We have observed a critical and persistent issue in our distributed and low-level training pipeline where two identical training runs launched with the exact same initial weights, dataset ordering, and random seeds produce diverging weight trajectories after only a few dozen steps. This nondeterminism severely impacts our ability to reproduce experimental results, debug convergence anomalies, and verify gradient correctness in our custom FlashAttention backward kernels and stochastic dropout layers. Profiling indicates that atomic accumulation operations in the gradient reduction phase and non-reentrant pseudorandom number generators in dropout are prime suspects. When atomic additions are executed concurrently across thread blocks, the floating-point addition order becomes non-associative and dependent on thread scheduling race conditions, leading to subtle bit-level discrepancies in weight updates. Furthermore, the lack of strict seed tracking and state synchronization across layers exacerbates this divergence. Your objective in this unit is to systematically isolate the sources of nondeterminism, enforce strict state locking for random number generators, substitute non-associative atomic paths with deterministic reduction strategies or ordered atomic sequences, verify identical weight trajectories across multiple training runs, quantify the performance overhead introduced by deterministic execution modes, and expose a clean configuration flag to toggle between fast nondeterministic execution and fully reproducible deterministic training without regressions.
