We are observing severe scaling bottlenecks when training large multi-layer transformer networks across distributed worker nodes in our cluster. During profiling of our tensor parallelism pipelines, the linear layers handling the column-parallel and row-parallel partitions of the multi-layer perceptron block exhibit unexpected latency spikes and synchronization stalls. Specifically, the communication overhead during the gather and all-reduce phases scales poorly as we increase the tensor parallel world size, causing severe gradient synchronization delays and lowering our overall hardware utilization well below expected thresholds.

Engineers report that our current implementations lack a rigorous, from-scratch realization of DTensor column-parallel and row-parallel matrix multiplication primitives. Without precise modeling of the per-layer communication volume formulas—accounting for sequence lengths, hidden dimensions, intermediate activation sizes, and the exact count of collective communication bytes transferred during forward and backward passes—we cannot accurately diagnose whether our collective primitives are operating at peak efficiency or if redundant inter-GPU broadcasts are inflating our communication footprint. Furthermore, our development environment lacks a clean, numpy-based reference implementation of the tensor parallel MLP block that can faithfully simulate the exact slicing, local matrix multiplications, and communication operators (such as the f and g operators in Megatron-LM style tensor parallelism) without relying on heavy external runtime frameworks. We need a robust, standalone verification suite that implements these primitives from first principles, computes accurate communication volumes, and validates local tensor mechanics under strict mathematical invariants.
