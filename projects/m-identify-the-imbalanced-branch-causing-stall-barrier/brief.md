Ticket: #4092 - Kernel Execution Stalling in Compute Phase Due to Synchronization Bottlenecks

We have observed severe performance degradation in our primary recurrent reduction kernel during large-scale batched inference runs. Profiling logs generated from recent hardware performance counter snapshots indicate that execution efficiency drops significantly during the intermediate reduction loops. Specifically, thread warps spend an abnormally high proportion of their active cycles sitting in a stalled state rather than executing math or memory instructions.

Initial investigations show that the dominant stall reason reported by the scheduler instrumentation is `stall_barrier`, indicating that warps are frequently blocked waiting at synchronization points or instruction barriers. However, the exact source of this imbalance across thread execution paths remains unclear. Engineers report that some branches within the reduction loops execute divergent workloads, potentially causing threads within the same CTA to reach synchronization barriers at drastically different times.

Furthermore, attempts to remove unnecessary synchronization primitives like `__syncthreads()` in un-aliased regions have yielded mixed performance results. We need a systematic mechanism to pinpoint which specific branch divergence causes the `stall_barrier` dominance, and to rigorously verify whether a proposed synchronization removal correlates correctly with the observed drop in stall counts and corresponding speedup metrics, without violating safety invariants. Please build the analysis and verification modules to diagnose and test these stall characteristics.
