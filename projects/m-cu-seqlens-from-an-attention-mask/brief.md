We are observing intermittent failures and severe degradation in our variable-length sequence handling pipeline when processing packed multi-document inputs in FlashAttention-compatible kernels. During high-throughput inference runs under heavy load, downstream attention outputs occasionally exhibit corrupted token alignments, and latency spikes occur due to unexpected tensor shape mismatches. Engineers have reported that when batching multiple distinct text prompts into a single continuous sequence, the cumulative sequence lengths vector (`cu_seqlens`) fails to accurately reflect the true boundary transitions between adjacent documents, leading to incorrect block-sparse indexing. Furthermore, round-trip conversions between padded batch tensors and packed unpadded representations are dropping tokens or introducing phantom padding tokens at document boundaries, destroying data integrity. Most alarmingly, telemetry indicates potential cross-document information leakage during attention computation where tokens from one customer's prompt inappropriately attend to tokens in a separate, adjacent document within the same packed tensor buffer. We need a robust implementation that correctly derives cumulative sequence lengths from attention masks, guarantees a lossless pad/unpad round trip, and rigorously detects or prevents cross-document leakage before these kernels are deployed to production environments.
