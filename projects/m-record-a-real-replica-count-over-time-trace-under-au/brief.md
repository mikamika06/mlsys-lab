We are observing erratic resource utilization patterns in our production Ray Serve deployment under fluctuating request loads. During peak hours, the autoscaler appears to continuously scale worker replicas up and down within very short time windows, sometimes oscillating between minimum and maximum replica limits multiple times per minute. This rapid fluctuation is causing severe latency spikes, intermittent request timeouts, and unnecessary overhead on our underlying node clusters. While our theoretical queue-length models predict a smooth, gradual scaling curve responding to incoming traffic volume, the actual recorded replica-count-over-time trace reveals chaotic behavior and frequent thrashing. Operators are currently unable to reliably audit replica allocation history or distinguish between legitimate traffic surges and unstable scaling configurations. We need a robust framework to accurately parse replica-count traces, quantify the divergence between theoretical predictions and real-world Ray autoscaling behavior, and automatically detect autoscaler thrashing from logged trace events so we can stabilize our deployment parameters.
