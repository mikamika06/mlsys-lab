Our high-throughput inference system is preparing to ingest deep, highly pruned large language model checkpoints where parameter matrices contain extensive blocks of zeros. Currently, our pipeline loads these weights directly into dense FP16 arrays, resulting in massive memory wastage, severe bandwidth saturation on memory-bound GPU kernels, and inefficient PCIe transfer times during model parallel initialization across multi-node clusters.

We need an optimized sparse tensor representation format that serializes non-zero values alongside a compact bitmask to designate element presence within fixed-size blocks or flattened grids. Without a proper encoding and decoding layer, we cannot verify exact round-trip reconstruction integrity, nor can we compute the exact theoretical break-even sparsity point where the bitmask overhead plus compressed values array matches the footprint of a raw dense tensor. Furthermore, downstream engineers lack empirical visibility into actual byte-size savings across progressive sparsity levels ranging from 10% to 99%, making it impossible to establish operational thresholds for when to switch between dense and sparse kernel paths in our serving runtime. Your task is to implement the complete encoding, decoding, break-even analysis, and byte-savings measurement utility modules, ensuring all components adhere to strict validation checks and robust regression testing.
