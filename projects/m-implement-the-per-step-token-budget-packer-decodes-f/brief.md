Serving engines running large language models frequently experience severe tail-latency spikes and erratic token generation times during mixed prefill and decode workloads. When long prompts arrive concurrently with ongoing token generation steps, the request scheduler stalls active decode requests for tens of milliseconds while processing raw, unchunked prefill blocks. This resource contention causes severe jitter in Inter-Token Latency (ITL) metrics, leading to noticeable micro-stutters and throughput degradation for end users despite an available compute token budget per step.

To resolve this issue, the serving engine requires a per-step token-budget packer that prioritizes decode requests first, allocating remaining capacity to sliced prefill chunks. Additionally, accurate mathematical modeling of step counts for arbitrary prefill lengths under specific budgets, alongside a predictive ITL jitter model, is essential to quantify and mitigate these latency spikes.
