We have observed a significant drop in downstream task accuracy after performing magnitude pruning on our core classification models deployed in production. While the compression stage successfully zeroes out redundant weights and reduces memory footprints, the resulting sparse models exhibit a severe degradation in performance, failing to meet our baseline service level objectives. Engineering teams have attempted standard fine-tuning using cross-entropy loss on the downstream dataset, but recovery is painfully slow, often requiring thousands of update steps to claw back acceptable accuracy levels, and frequently plateauing well below the original unpruned model's capabilities. Furthermore, initial explorations into knowledge distillation using the original unpruned model as a teacher have shown promise, yet our current pipeline lacks a robust, automated harness to quantify and compare the efficiency of plain recovery versus distillation-based recovery. Specifically, we lack a reliable way to compute precise step counts required to reach 90 percent accuracy recovery under different training configurations, leading to guesswork in hyperparameter tuning and resource allocation for our training clusters. We need a modular implementation that clearly formalizes both plain and distillation-based recovery training steps, evaluates their convergence behavior accurately, and enforces strict regression testing to ensure our recovery loss formulations and step-to-recovery calculations remain correct and robust across iterations.
