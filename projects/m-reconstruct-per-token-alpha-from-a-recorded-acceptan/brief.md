We are observing anomalies in our speculative decoding pipeline where the effective per-token speedup (often denoted as alpha or the average accepted tokens per draft step) does not match our aggregate throughput metrics. When we run speculative decoding with small-to-medium draft models over large evaluation suites, our downstream inference servers log a complete histogram of recorded acceptance lengths (i.e., how many tokens were accepted per draft generation step). However, our current monitoring tools only display the raw distribution counts or simple arithmetic means of accepted tokens per draft step, which completely obscures the underlying per-token acceptance probability distributions and makes it impossible to accurately model the marginal utility of adding extra draft steps or tuning speculative budgets under variable latency constraints.

Your task is to build a robust analysis module that takes a recorded histogram of acceptance lengths from speculative decoding runs, implements the mathematical reconstruction of the per-token acceptance probabilities (alpha parameters), and builds a regression test suite to safeguard these calculations against structural degradation or statistical skew. You will need to implement a clean library structure that handles histogram normalization, calculates individual token acceptance probabilities via expectation-maximization or analytical inversion from cumulative acceptance frequencies, and validates your invariants under various simulated generation profiles. Ensure that your functions handle edge cases such as zero-length acceptances, long-tail truncated histograms, and floating-point stability gracefully without introducing performance bottlenecks or side-effect mutations.
