Our distributed training and compilation pipeline has run into a severe operational bottleneck in multi-process environments. When executing distributed runs across multiple worker nodes and isolated subprocesses, compilation times spike unexpectedly during startup, severely degrading cluster utilization and delaying job launch times. Operators have observed that despite pre-generating and persisting compiled execution graphs, downstream worker processes fail to reuse these cached artifacts, forcing redundant and expensive full recompilations from scratch. Furthermore, minor configuration adjustments across job submissions routinely invalidate existing cache keys, but engineers have no reliable automated way to pinpoint precisely which configuration field triggered the cache miss and forced a cold compilation path. This creates massive latency overheads, unpredictable scaling behavior, and wasted compute cycles across our machine learning infrastructure clusters. We need a robust, process-safe artifact saving and loading mechanism, precise measurement of cold versus warm compile cycles, and deterministic identification of the exact configuration fields responsible for breaking cache keys.
