Our multi-head attention layers are currently executing at maximum capacity across all production serving instances, leading to unacceptably high serving latency and increased memory bandwidth pressure during peak traffic windows. Profiling logs reveal that downstream throughput drops significantly whenever large transformer models process long context lengths, causing queue build-ups and timeout errors in client applications. Although hardware acceleration is fully utilized, raw execution times remain bottlenecked by attention compute overhead rather than feed-forward layers. Initial investigations indicate that certain attention heads contribute negligibly to overall model accuracy and output representation, yet they consume proportional compute cycles and memory bandwidth during every forward pass. Engineers urgently need a systematic approach to quantify individual attention head contributions using expected sensitivity metrics, determine optimal greedy removal sequences based on these importance scores, and accurately measure real-world latency reductions achieved by pruning low-importance heads. Without a robust, automated pipeline to identify, rank, and prune redundant heads safely without catastrophic accuracy loss, our deployment clusters will continue to suffer from suboptimal resource utilization and latency degradation under heavy load.
