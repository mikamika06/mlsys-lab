When running large language models in quantized int8 precision via bitsandbytes LLM.int8(), we occasionally observe unexpected numerical degradation or catastrophic outlier behavior in specific layers. The standard mixed-precision decomposition relies on isolating extreme activation outliers—values exceeding a specific magnitude threshold—and keeping them in high-precision fp16 while quantizing the rest of the matrix multiplication to int8.

Our current deployment pipeline implements a naive int8 quantization over the entire model without accounting for these planted outliers, causing severe perplexity spikes on specific benchmark sequences. Furthermore, when attempting to introduce a threshold-based decomposed int8 approach, we lack a principled way to balance precision preservation against our strict floating-point operations constraints. Specifically, under a hard fp16-FLOP cap, we need to correctly identify and isolate the exact linear modules that accumulate these high-magnitude outliers and must therefore be explicitly skipped or treated with specialized mixed-precision decomposition. Without this mechanism, the system either exceeds the allocated computational budget or fails to mitigate the extreme error propagation caused by untamed outliers. Your task is to implement the reference routines that correctly select the optimal decomposition threshold, identify the exact modules requiring int8-skip behavior, and execute the decomposed versus naive int8 comparison on matrices with planted outliers.
