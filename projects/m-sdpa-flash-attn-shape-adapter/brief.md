Engineers working on low-level ML systems frequently encounter unexpected shape mismatches and subtle numerical divergence when integrating high-performance FlashAttention kernels into standard PyTorch training and inference workflows. PyTorch's native Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDPA) interface relies on standard contiguous batch-head-sequence-head_dim layouts and specific scaling conventions, whereas FlashAttention expects interleaved or specific sequence-major tensor layouts alongside precise internal log-sum-exp (LSE) reduction semantics.

When developers attempt to swap out standard attention routines for FlashAttention or wrap them with custom Python APIs, intermittent bugs arise from incorrect dimension permutations, missing scaling factors, or improperly computed LSE tensors used for backward passes and log-probability calculations. These issues manifest as gradient corruption, numerical instability under mixed precision, or assertion errors during integration and regression testing.

Your task is to build a robust shape adapter and a pure-numpy reference attention implementation that precisely matches FlashAttention semantics, including the exact LSE definition. You will also implement a comprehensive regression test suite in `tests/test_regression.py` that validates tensor shape roundtrips, scaling factors, and numerical stability across diverse input configurations, ensuring reliable interoperability between standard SDPA conventions and FlashAttention kernels.
