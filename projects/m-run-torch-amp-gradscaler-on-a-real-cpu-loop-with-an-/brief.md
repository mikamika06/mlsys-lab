We are observing unexpected numerical divergence and loss explosions during mixed-precision fine-tuning runs on our local development nodes. Specifically, when executing custom CPU-emulated training loops with `torch.amp.GradScaler`, downstream parameter updates behave erratically whenever sudden gradient overflow occurs. In several evaluation runs, encountering an infinite or NaN gradient value either causes the internal scale factor to lock up permanently or fails to properly suppress weight updates on steps where gradients are invalid. Furthermore, comparing our fp16 scaling behavior against native bf16 runs reveals discrepancies in magnitude progression that undermine convergence stability under high gradient variance. Engineers report that models trained under these mixed-precision loops intermittently produce corrupted weights or fail to recover once an overflow event is triggered, leading to NaN outputs during validation epochs. We need a robust implementation of the training loop utilizing `GradScaler` that correctly handles gradient infs, guarantees that skipped optimization steps leave weights completely untouched, and accurately benchmarks scale evolution against bf16 baselines.
