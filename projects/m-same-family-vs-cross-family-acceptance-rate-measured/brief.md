We are running speculative decoding loops with draft and target model pairings on local hardware, and the system is stalling or underperforming because we do not know how the acceptance rate shifts when pairing models from the same model family versus cross-family models. Right now, our speculative execution engine treats all draft models uniformly, applying a fixed lookahead length and assuming equivalent token verification probabilities regardless of whether the draft model shares architecture, tokenizer, and pretraining lineage with the target model or comes from an entirely distinct family. In practice, cross-family draft models frequently diverge in their token prediction distributions, leading to low acceptance rates, wasted compute cycles on aborted draft verification passes, and suboptimal generation speedups. We need a measurement harness to systematically compute, log, and analyze the token acceptance rates for same-family versus cross-family draft and target model pairings under identical local generation workloads, ensuring our speculative scheduling logic can dynamically adapt or reject inefficient pairings before execution.
