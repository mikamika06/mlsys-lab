We are investigating a Mixture of Experts (MoE) routing pipeline where tokens are dispatched to a set of sparse expert feed-forward networks. In this regime, each token is routed to its top-k experts based on router logits, but each expert has a strict capacity limit determined by the expert capacity factor. When more tokens are routed to an expert than its allocated capacity, the excess tokens are dropped and do not receive expert computations, leading to dropped tokens and potentially degraded model perplexity.

During recent profiling runs on our long-context MoE models, we noticed an unexpected discrepancy between our token-drop rate metrics and the theoretical capacity limits. Specifically, when we implement grouped-GEMM token dispatch to batch computations across experts, the outputs occasionally diverge from a naive per-expert loop implementation under specific padding and token-sorting permutations. Furthermore, our empirical token-drop fractions measured on real router outputs do not match the expected theoretical binomial tail bounds when capacity factors are scaled down for low-latency inference.

Your task is to implement the expert capacity calculation and token-drop fraction derivation, ensure that the grouped-GEMM token dispatch mathematically matches the naive per-expert loop reference, and write a comprehensive regression test suite that catches any divergence in the token sorting or capacity masking logic.
