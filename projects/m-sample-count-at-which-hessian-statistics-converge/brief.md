An active optimization workflow for low-level machine learning libraries is failing to reach stable quantization convergence because the calibration routines exhibit high variance across different text distribution sets. When quantization calibration relies on unstructured sample sets, the resulting Hessian statistics fluctuate wildly depending on whether the input sequences match the downstream domain distribution or incorporate out-of-domain conversational noise. Engineers investigating weight-only and mixed-precision quantization setups notice that minor perturbations in the calibration loader sequence order yield unpredictable scaling factors and severe layer-wise SNR degradation. Furthermore, naive loaders fail to determine the exact sample count threshold where the empirical Fisher information matrix and second-order Hessian approximations actually stabilize, causing unnecessary compute overhead or premature truncation of the calibration set. The objective is to design a robust calibration harness featuring a deterministic sample loader, domain-matched vs mismatched validation routines, and a rigorous convergence tracking module that precisely identifies the sample threshold where Hessian statistics converge within acceptable error bounds.
