Production traces for our continuous-batching vLLM serving engine show unexpected degradation when traffic scales up. While average request latency looks superficially acceptable under moderate loads, clients reporting strict Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) metrics under a 2-second SLO are experiencing severe tail-drop and silent drops in throughput. Furthermore, our offline log aggregation scripts produce running batch size curves that diverge completely from expected hardware utilization traces, leading to erroneous capacity planning. Compounding this, attempts to validate system health using standard queueing theory relationships—specifically Little's Law relating queue length, arrival rate, and latency—yield massive discrepancies that drift wildly depending on whether the server is saturated or idle. We need to implement robust log reconstruction, accurate Little's Law verification, and precise goodput computation under the 2s TTFT SLO to correctly track capacity limits and diagnose serving bottlenecks.
