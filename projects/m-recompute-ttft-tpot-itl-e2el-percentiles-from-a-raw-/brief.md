We are experiencing a series of regressions and instrumentation inaccuracies in our vLLM benchmarking and serving metrics pipeline, specifically within our benchmark harness and methodology track. During recent high-throughput load tests against our serving engine, the reported latency percentiles (TTFT, TPOT, ITL, and E2EL) for concurrent requests have shown significant drift compared to raw trace logs. Upon manual inspection of the raw bench results JSON output, we noticed that our percentile computations are either using naive linear approximations or failing to properly isolate warm-up traffic from steady-state measurements.

Compounding this issue, our traffic generation module has failed to accurately reflect production-grade request dynamics. Specifically, the Poisson arrival generator does not properly enforce `--request-rate` semantics under heavy concurrency, leading to bursty arrival patterns that skew queue depths and invalidate our concurrency stress tests. Furthermore, when pushing the serving engine to saturation, our current harness conflates open-loop and closed-loop arrival modes. We are currently unable to reliably demonstrate or measure how open-loop and closed-loop systems diverge at saturation—specifically how open-loop systems exhibit queue explosions while closed-loop systems maintain bounded queues at the cost of artificially suppressed concurrency and hidden latency inflation.

Your task is to build a robust metrics recomputation and workload generation suite that correctly computes percentiles from raw JSON traces, implements a mathematically rigorous Poisson request arrival generator adhering to standard rate parameters, and provides a clear comparative harness demonstrating open versus closed-loop dynamics at saturation. Ensure all tests and harnesses strictly adhere to our automated verification requirements.
