An automated performance regression suite running on a staging compiler cluster has flagged a series of scheduler inconsistencies when applying compound TVM TensorIR transformations, specifically involving loop transformations like splitting with factor $k$ followed by reordering, as well as target-specific instruction vectorization passes.

During our routine validation of lowering sequences for low-level machine learning kernels, developers have noticed that manually computing loop bounds, strides, and extents after a compound split-and-reorder transformation frequently leads to out-of-bound memory accesses or compiler assertion faults. Furthermore, when attempting to vectorize specific inner loops that contain hidden or indirect data dependencies across iteration variables, the compiler fails to lower the primitive correctly and instead raises an opaque TVM ScheduleError rather than cleanly catching and diagnosing the invalid optimization attempt.

Your task is to implement a robust compiler analysis module that accurately derives the resulting loop extents for multi-level nested loops after applying a split factor $k$ and reorder primitive, verify these mathematically against actual TVM schedule executions, and construct a comprehensive test harness that precisely intercepts and validates TVM ScheduleErrors triggered by illegal vectorization over data-dependent memory regions.
