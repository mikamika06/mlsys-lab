# Ticket: Medusa Head Training and Speculative Sampling Discrepancies

We are observing unexpected evaluation behaviors when deploying multi-head speculative decoding schemes, specifically with $K=2$ Medusa heads trained on top of a frozen small language model backbone. During offline evaluation runs, our speculative decoding loop is reporting erratic speedups and inconsistent accepted-length statistics compared to baseline single-token generation.

Specifically, when switching between standard greedy typical acceptance sampling and strict rejection sampling mechanics, the average accepted token length diverges significantly from theoretical expectations, sometimes collapsing to near zero under heavy load or high entropy distributions. Furthermore, when benchmarking our trained second Medusa head (head-2) top-1 token classification accuracy against published literature benchmarks for similar-sized Vicuna models (which typically hover around sixty percent accuracy on standard validation splits), our internal pipeline yields numbers that are either suspiciously low or completely untracked.

Engineers are reporting that training loss curves stabilize normally during the initial head training phase, yet the downstream speculative generation script fails to replicate expected latency gains, hinting at potential mismatch in logits alignment, residual connection application, or temperature scaling during the acceptance phase. We need to implement clean, reproducible modules for Medusa head training, comparative speculative sampling evaluations, and robust regression tests to isolate and fix these discrepancies.
