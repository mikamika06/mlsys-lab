Our low-level edge model export pipeline (`rw2-edge-export`) has been encountering frequent failures when deploying models with non-default or variable input shapes. Specifically, while models exported with a fixed batch size or default sequence length execute correctly, any attempt by downstream applications to supply a custom shape or dynamically scale the input dimensions results in immediate rejection during the lowering phase or catastrophic kernel faults at runtime.

When our teams attempt to mitigate this by enumerating multiple discrete input shapes to avoid full graph recompilation for every tiny variation, the compile times scale exponentially and swamp our build worker pools. Furthermore, our intermediate representation lacks a dependable symbolic-dimension propagation checker, meaning dynamic ranges (such as RangeDim constraints versus discrete EnumeratedShapes) are frequently misaligned across fusion boundaries. This leads to silent buffer overflows or invalid memory strides in downstream tensor operators.

We need to overhaul the core shape verification, symbolic dimension tracking, and compilation budgeting logic. Your task is to implement the necessary modules in the edge export library to properly validate flexible shapes, check symbolic propagation paths across operators, and safely manage compile-time growth when dealing with enumerated shape variants.
