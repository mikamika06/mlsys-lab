Our continuous batching engine is suffering from severe throughput degradation and tail-latency spikes whenever requests with very long output token limits are mixed into the workload. Under heavy load, a handful of requests requesting large generation lengths (e.g., 2048+ tokens) capture continuous batch slots for extended periods, starving shorter requests and causing head-of-line blocking in the scheduling queue.

When observing the batch turnover rate, we notice that active batch occupancy remains high, but the rate of completed requests drops sharply. The scheduler continues to allocate new continuous batch slots to incoming long-output requests without accounting for the remaining generation budget or token capacity overhead, leading to severe batch stagnation. We need a mechanism to analyze, model, and mitigate the effect of long-output requests on batch turnover, ensuring fair scheduling, bounded request latency, and optimal resource utilization across mixed workloads.
