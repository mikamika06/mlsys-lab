Our serving telemetry has been showing an intermittent tail-latency spike that is dragging down our service level objectives under high traffic. When users send concurrent chat completion streams, the p99 latency shoots up from a stable baseline of 45 milliseconds to over 800 milliseconds, while average throughput appears stable or even slightly degraded.

We need to perform metrics forensics to figure out whether these tail latency excursions are driven by request queueing at the front door or block preemption events inside the continuous batching engine. Specifically, we need to inspect the engine's internal instrumentation counters—such as allocation stalls, block eviction counts, and queue wait times—under synthetic load tests. Once we can isolate and count preemption events directly, we need a mechanism to analytically determine the exact `max_num_seqs` configuration parameter that guarantees zero preemption at a given target queries-per-second (QPS) workload, preventing cache thrashing and latency degradation before it impacts production users.
