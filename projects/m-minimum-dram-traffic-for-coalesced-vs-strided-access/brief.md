Performance counters on a newly profiled memory-intensive kernel show terrible efficiency during execution. The memory bandwidth utilization reported by the hardware hardware profiler sits at a fraction of peak capacity, despite memory load operations appearing completely straightforward in the source code. Specifically, when accessing multi-dimensional tensors with non-unit strides, execution stalls significantly longer than expected during global memory transactions. Preliminary logs indicate that transactions are ballooning out of proportion compared to the nominal byte count requested by the threads, but the exact amplification factor remains unclear. We urgently need diagnostic tools to analytically compute the theoretical minimum DRAM traffic, simulate the exact 32-byte sector coalescing behavior of a warp given 32 independent per-thread addresses, and automatically quantify the excess-traffic ratio of real strided kernels versus coalesced ones. Without these utilities, we cannot pinpoint why certain access patterns degrade memory sub-system performance or validate whether optimization efforts successfully reclaim bandwidth.
