# Ticket: Latency Instability and Unpredictable SLO Violations under Mixed Serving Workloads

Engineering teams managing our large language model inference infrastructure are facing severe operational instability during peak traffic hours. Production clusters running vLLM are exhibiting erratic performance patterns where user-facing applications experience sudden, unpredictable latency spikes. Specifically, when serving a heterogeneous workload consisting of long-context document summarization prompts interleaved with fast, streaming chat completions, the system's performance degrades in conflicting directions.

Operators have attempted to mitigate these issues by tuning the global `--max-num-batched-tokens` parameter. However, manual adjustments have proven ineffective and frustrating. Increasing the token budget improves steady-state token generation speed for active streams but causes catastrophic head-of-line blocking for incoming requests, inflating Time to First Token (TTFT) to unacceptable levels. Conversely, lowering the budget curbs prefill congestion and improves TTFT for new arrivals, but severely throttles decode throughput, driving up Inter-Token Latency (ITL) and causing noticeable text generation stutter for streaming clients.

Because these two critical metrics operate in direct opposition, changing configuration values feels entirely like guesswork. We lack a rigorous, systematic methodology to explore the design space. We urgently require a programmatic sweep utility that simulates our exact server workload across various `--max-num-batched-tokens` configurations, captures the resulting performance trade-offs, and extracts the optimal Pareto front curve. This will allow platform engineers to make data-driven decisions aligned with our strict latency Service Level Objectives rather than relying on trial and error.
