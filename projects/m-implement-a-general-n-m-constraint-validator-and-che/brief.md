We are experiencing erratic behavior and silent correctness failures when dealing with 2:4 semi-structured sparsity weight matrices in our low-level inference pipeline. During weight loading and kernel preparation, operators report success even when weight blocks violate the fundamental N:M structural constraint (specifically, having more than N non-zero elements out of any contiguous M elements along the constrained dimension).

Engineers have observed that naive masking or direct slicing from pruned checkpoints often leaves malformed blocks that slip past initial sanity checks. When these corrupted weights are dispatched to specialized hardware execution units or low-level accelerated kernels expecting strict 2:4 sparse layouts, it triggers illegal hardware instruction faults, catastrophic numerical outputs, or silent memory corruption. We need a general, robust constraint validator that can inspect arbitrary weight tensors, verify the N:M sparsity invariant precisely across arbitrary blocks, extract real operational masks, and reject invalid layouts before they ever reach the execution pipeline. Furthermore, we need a rigorous regression testing framework to ensure our validation logic correctly identifies structural violations without producing false positives on well-formed sparse matrices.
