Every time we deploy a new version of our machine learning inference service to production, our monitoring dashboards light up with alerts. The deployment process itself goes smoothly, container images are built and pushed, orchestration rolls out the new pods without errors, and health checks return green immediately. However, the very first user requests hitting the newly minted pods experience severe tail latency spikes, often timing out or stalling for nearly a minute and a half before finally returning a response. Subsequent requests to the same endpoints complete instantaneously, running at optimal throughput and low latency, but that initial penalty hits our customer-facing SLAs hard during every rolling update or auto-scaling event. Our infrastructure engineers have observed high CPU utilization spikes concentrated in backend compilation components right after pod initialization, but the exact root cause and propagation path of this behavior remain elusive across our distributed serving nodes. We need a systematic approach to identify, isolate, cache, and safely manage these compilation artifacts across machines and deployments so that our systems can handle traffic smoothly from the very first incoming query without suffering from severe startup latency degradations.
