Our distributed model training runs are currently experiencing unexpected memory exhaustion and mysterious gradient divergences when attempting to leverage PyTorch's native activation checkpointing framework. In our multi-layer transformer pipelines, we rely on checkpointing intermediate activations during the forward pass to conserve scarce GPU memory, subsequently recomputing them during backward propagation. However, engineers have observed that specific selective checkpoint strategies result in silent numerical drift or exactness mismatches compared to non-checkpointed runs, while other configurations fail to yield the expected memory savings because transient autograd tensors leak across boundaries. Additionally, when we attempt to profile the memory-versus-recompute-time Pareto curve to find the optimal trade-off point for our cluster hardware, our manual heuristics fail to accurately capture the true operator-level breakdown of full versus selective recomputation costs. We urgently require a systematic module to verify gradient exactness, map empirical memory-versus-recompute trade-off curves, and analyze real operator breakdowns under various checkpoint strategies to guarantee stable and efficient training runs.
