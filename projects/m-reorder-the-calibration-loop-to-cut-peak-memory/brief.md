When running post-training calibration or activation quantization loops on large language models under tight memory constraints, engineers frequently encounter unexpected Out-Of-Memory (OOM) crashes even when the model weights themselves comfortably fit within the available accelerator or system RAM. During typical calibration passes, intermediate activations, hooked tensor buffers, and calibration datasets accumulate or are allocated sequentially across forward passes, often peaking at unpredictable thresholds depending on how data loaders and module hooks iterate over batches and layers.

In this lab, you are investigating a quantization calibration pipeline for low-level ML systems where the primary bottleneck is peak Resident Set Size (RSS) and device memory during sample calibration. The naive calibration loop iterates sequentially through batches while keeping large intermediate tensor caches or multiple layer outputs pinned or resident simultaneously, driving peak memory consumption far beyond the steady-state footprint.

Your task is to diagnose and refactor the calibration execution order, determine the maximum workable number of calibration samples that can be safely processed without triggering memory pressure limits, and accurately measure and verify the sequential-onloading peak RSS behavior. You will restructure the calibration flow to minimize peak memory, implement proper resource cleanup and buffer reuse, ensure that calibration sample limits are dynamically bounded by available budget, and write rigorous regression tests to safeguard your memory-efficient implementation against future regressions.
