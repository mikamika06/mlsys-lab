Specifically, when trying a naive early-exit probe that reuses the frozen final unembedding matrix at intermediate layer $k$, token agreement metrics fluctuate wildly depending on whether normalization layers, scaling factors, or precise layer-wise hidden state alignments are correctly accounted for across validation batches. Furthermore, sweeping through multiple exit depths to establish a depth-versus-agreement tradeoff curve requires precise comparison against published empirical baselines from LayerSkip literature, where untrained early-exit features frequently exhibit sharp drops in top-1 agreement at shallower layers. Without rigorous automated test suites and standardized checking harnesses, subtle bugs in matrix product orientations, tensor shape broadcasting, or error aggregation can go unnoticed, leading to invalid conclusions about model representational maturity and premature deployment choices.
