We are optimizing elementwise and fused pointwise Triton kernels in our low-level machine learning systems pipeline. During recent profiling runs, we noticed that while kernel fusion is generally expected to reduce memory bandwidth bottlenecks, the wall-time measurements obtained via `do_bench` can sometimes show unexpected regressions or minimal speedups depending on the grid configuration, block sizes, and intermediate memory allocations.

Your task is to analyze recorded Triton benchmarking traces and determine whether a given fused kernel implementation achieves a sufficient performance ratio compared to its unfused baseline. Specifically, you need to parse recorded benchmarking outputs, compute the throughput and wall-time ratios across various problem sizes, and implement a robust verification workflow in Python. You must ensure that your analysis correctly identifies speedups and flags any regressions or unstable configurations where the fused kernel underperforms relative to the unfused equivalent.
