# Symptom: Draft Model Training Discrepancy Between Teacher-Forced Loss and Inference Acceptance Rate

Our speculative decoding pipeline has been training draft models using standard teacher-forced cross-entropy loss over target prefix sequences. While the training loss steadily decreases and per-token perplexity improves across evaluation epochs, the actual inference-time speedup remains stagnant or even degrades when deploying the trained draft models with target verification models.

During multi-step speculative generation rollouts, where the draft model proposes a sequence of candidate tokens autoregressively before the target model verifies them in parallel, we observe frequent rejections early in the draft rollout chain. Although individual token prediction accuracy under teacher forcing appears high, the cumulative acceptance rate across multi-step draft blocks does not correlate with the expected speedups predicted by validation loss metrics.

Engineers report that models with lower cross-entropy loss occasionally exhibit worse multi-step acceptance rates during deployment than older checkpoints. Standard cross-entropy optimization assumes ground-truth prefix contexts and evaluates single-step next-token conditional distributions independently, ignoring the compound error propagation and acceptance criteria inherent in multi-step speculative verification schemes. We need a robust evaluation and objective framework that accurately models training-time test vs teacher-forced discrepancies, computes true multi-step acceptance probabilities, and ensures our optimization pipelines reliably reflect real inference performance.
