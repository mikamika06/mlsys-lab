We are observing severe tail latency degradation and suboptimal hardware utilization when deploying our exported edge model onto embedded multi-core processors. During recent performance profiling sessions, engineers noticed that simply cranking up the OpenMP or native thread count to the maximum available hardware cores does not yield the best p95 latency. Instead, contention on shared last-level caches and memory bandwidth bottlenecks often cause p95 latencies to spike unpredictably at high concurrency levels. Furthermore, when summing individual operator execution times from our profiling harness, we observe a massive discrepancy compared to actual end-to-end wall-clock execution time, hinting at heavy scheduler overhead, synchronization barriers, or unmodeled dispatch stalls. Finally, when attempting to maximize request throughput under a strict end-to-end latency SLA, static thread allocations fail to adapt, leading to SLA violations or underutilized compute capacity. We need a systematic software module that performs a precise thread-count sweep to locate the true minimum p95 latency configuration, quantifies the exact gap between summed operator profiles and wall-clock execution, and computes the maximum achievable throughput under a given latency service level agreement without violating our established performance invariants.
