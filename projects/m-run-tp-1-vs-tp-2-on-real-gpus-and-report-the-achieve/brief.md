Our vLLM inference cluster deployment team has reported unexpected performance anomalies when evaluating tensor parallelism configurations on multi-GPU nodes. Specifically, during recent profiling runs utilizing Llama-style transformer models on dual-GPU nodes, scaling from a tensor parallel degree of one (TP=1) to two (TP=2) yields erratic throughput ratios that deviate significantly from theoretical linear speedup expectations. While single-GPU execution runs complete without anomalies, configuring distributed tensor parallelism across two devices results in either degraded token generation rates or inconsistent inter-GPU communication overheads that invalidate our capacity planning models. Engineers are currently unable to reliably quantify the exact scaling efficiency or verify whether communication bottlenecks stem from synchronous collective operations, tensor slice partitioning mismatches, or pipeline latency in the underlying serving engine harness. We need a robust measurement and analysis pipeline that systematically executes or simulates TP=1 and TP=2 benchmarks, extracts core throughput metrics under controlled synthetic workloads, accurately computes the achieved scaling efficiency ratio, and enforces strict regression invariants to prevent silent performance regressions during subsequent serving infrastructure upgrades across production nodes.
