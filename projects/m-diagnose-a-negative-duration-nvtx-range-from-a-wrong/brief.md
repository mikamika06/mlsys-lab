We are investigating a persistent profiling anomaly in our production training pipeline. During a multi-node large language model pre-training run using mixed precision, our performance engineer noticed a bizarre and impossible metric: an NVTX range in the timeline visualization showing a strictly negative duration. The profiling export comes directly from NVIDIA Nsight Systems (nsys), capturing several iterations of transformer forward and backward passes.

When zooming into the trace around the step boundaries, a specific range annotated via the NVTX push/pop API appears to begin after it ends. This corruption invalidates our automated trace parsing tools, causing our post-processing pipelines to crash when calculating phase breakdowns. Further complicating matters, we also need to rank the top 5 `record_function`-instrumented phases by their self time using a companion trace gathered on an Apple Silicon device via PyTorch profiler.

Your task is to diagnose the negative duration NVTX range by finding the root cause—specifically identifying the incorrect thread pop operation that causes the range stack to mismatch—and write a robust analyzer that processes the SQLite or exported data structures. Additionally, you must implement the self-time ranking for the `record_function` phases from the Mac trace, ensuring all calculations handle nested scopes and inclusive-versus-exclusive time correctly. Finally, you will write a comprehensive regression test suite that fails if someone attempts to fix the NVTX range corruption by brute-force truncating or clamping durations instead of fixing the underlying mismatched thread context.
