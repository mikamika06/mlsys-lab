An unexpected and critical reliability incident occurred in our vLLM serving cluster during peak load, causing severe user-facing degradation and cascading service failures.

While monitoring live production traffic, our observability pipelines flagged a sudden spike in anomalous outputs. Instead of coherent natural language text, several active inference streams began continuously emitting a degenerate token sequence, dominated by endless repetitions of exclamation marks and associated looping punctuation markers. This runaway generation quickly saturated the remaining context window capacity, holding valuable KV cache blocks hostage and starving other concurrent requests of compute resources.

Shortly after the onset of this garbage output storm, the inference engine node experienced an abrupt fatal crash mid-batch due to severe out-of-memory errors and internal scheduling assertions. The abrupt termination dropped all in-flight requests that were currently being processed in the batch. Downstream client applications, relying on rigid timeout thresholds, experienced dropped connections and subsequently triggered aggressive automatic retries. This retry storm immediately hammered the surviving replicas with duplicate traffic, threatening a cascading total outage across the entire serving fleet.

Your engineering task is to investigate this failure mode thoroughly across three distinct phases. First, implement a robust detector function that parses generation outputs to accurately identify and isolate this specific garbage-output signature and repeated token degeneracy. Second, build an analytical tool to estimate the blast radius of the engine crash, computing the exact count of requests lost mid-batch and tracking the amplified retry load imposed on the system. Third, write a minimal, self-contained reproducer script and regression test suite based on this bug report, stripping away extraneous production noise to isolate the exact minimal failing configuration.
