When evaluating LLM serving traces under heavy concurrent load, aggregate performance metrics such as mean time-to-first-token (TTFT) or average time-per-output-token (TPOT) frequently appear within acceptable operational limits, yet user-facing service level objectives (SLOs) are systematically violated. Specifically, filtering execution results using combined thresholds such as `--goodput ttft:200,tpot:30` yields a success rate significantly lower than what independent metric averages would imply. Furthermore, actual end-to-end latency (E2EL) calculations diverge noticeably from the standard linear reconstruction formula defined as TTFT plus TPOT multiplied by the number of output tokens minus one. This discrepancy becomes pronounced during periods of execution preemption, resource contention, and scheduling delays. Additionally, computing inter-token latency (ITL) directly from raw token emission timestamps reveals sharp spikes and pauses that diverge substantially from steady-state TPOT estimates. Without a robust testing and analysis framework, diagnosing these subtle metric anomalies leads to silent regressions in serving quality evaluations. Learners must construct a rigorous metrics analysis module that accurately computes joint goodput semantics, quantifies preemption-induced E2EL gaps, and extracts divergent ITL versus TPOT characteristics from raw request traces.
