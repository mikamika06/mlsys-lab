We ran a big benchmarking script comparing MLX and llama.cpp-Metal across a range of models on our M3 Max machines. The numbers we published in the internal report show MLX being inexplicably slow—sometimes 2 to 3 times slower than llama.cpp even for 1000-token generations. A user on the internal issue tracker pointed out that if they just run the MLX generation loop twice in the REPL, the second time is radically faster. We suspect our benchmarking harness is naively averaging all runs, including the initial graph compilation and JIT trace that MLX performs on the first pass, thereby hopelessly skewing the reported tokens per second. Furthermore, some researchers are arguing that MLX is always faster in every scenario, but we specifically remember seeing llama.cpp edge it out slightly on very short generations like a 5-token chat response due to lower dispatch overhead. We need a harness that explicitly separates warmup runs from measured runs, quantifies exactly how much the naive average biases the result, and outputs a fair sequence-length matched comparison that can mathematically prove where each engine is actually slower.
