We are investigating a severe instability in our latest model training run. The training loss progresses smoothly for the first 300 steps, but suddenly diverges into NaNs. The only change between this run and our stable baseline is that we tried to optimize throughput by modifying our global precision settings. Someone enabled `torch.set_float32_matmul_precision('medium')` (TF32) on the cluster and also cast a variety of our custom projection blocks and normalization layers to `bfloat16`.

Nobody is sure if the divergence is caused by accumulated relative errors in our deep matrix multiplication chains running with fewer mantissa bits, or if specific layers we cast to bf16 are numerically unstable and suffering from extreme variance.

We need a reproducible toolset to evaluate exactly how much relative error accumulates when multiplying chains of matrices in these different precision modes (TF32 vs BF16). We also need a scanner that evaluates our sequence of layers, simulates a downcast to bfloat16 for both inputs and weights, and flags any layer where the relative error against a pure float32 reference spikes above a specified threshold. We cannot safely deploy this mixed-precision configuration until we know which layers are actually bf16-safe.
