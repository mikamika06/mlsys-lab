When integrating low-rank adaptation (LoRA) layers into production large language models for fine-tuning, engineering teams have observed intermittent numerical divergence or unexpected baseline shifts right at step 0 before any training gradients are even computed. During deployment rehearsals, pipeline validation scripts running initial inference passes show that wrapping certain linear projections with newly instantiated LoRA modules occasionally alters the output logits by tiny non-zero margins, even though standard initialization protocols dictate that the adapter should contribute precisely zero to the forward activation. This discrepancy causes downstream alignment and regression checks to fail because the initial model checkpoint combined with fresh adapters deviates from the frozen base weights. We need a rigorous verification utility and regression testsuite to inspect freshly initialized LoRA layers, ensuring that their parameters adhere strictly to zero-contribution invariants across various configurations and hidden dimensions before any backward passes or parameter updates occur.
