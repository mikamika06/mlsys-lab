We are observing subtle convergence failures and strange gradient behavior during distributed fine-tuning runs on memory-constrained cluster nodes. When scaling our batch size configurations via gradient accumulation, our training loss curves exhibit sharp, unexpected discontinuities, and in some cases, the model weights explode entirely during the initial steps.

Upon inspecting the trainer loop and step counters, the scaling factors applied during backward propagation appear inconsistent with the requested configurations. Specifically, jobs configured with large accumulation steps diverge significantly faster than baseline runs, even when the learning rate and effective batch size are held mathematically constant. Furthermore, while memory profiling confirms that our peak allocated VRAM remains entirely flat regardless of the accumulation multiplier, our per-step wall-clock duration scales linearly with the configured steps, creating severe bottlenecks in our multi-node synchronization pipelines.

We need to inspect our batch scaling logic, verify how accumulation parameters translate to step counts and effective sizes, analyze the gradient-magnitude inflation factors caused by missing loss normalizations or incorrect division counts across micro-batches, and confirm memory versus wall-clock performance invariants. The system needs to accurately compute required accumulation steps, derive gradient scaling corrections, and validate profiling behaviors via automated testing.
