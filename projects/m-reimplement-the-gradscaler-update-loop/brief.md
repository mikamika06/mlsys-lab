Engineers on the distributed training team have been investigating intermittent underflows and sudden gradient explosion spikes during mixed-precision training runs using FP16. While PyTorch's native `GradScaler` handles the scaling dynamics under the hood, custom profiling and lightweight debugging environments require a clean, transparent, pure-Python implementation of the loss-scaling loop to audit how scale factors adapt over time and how gradient norms dictate growth and backoff behavior.

Currently, when experimenting with non-standard optimizer step conditions or trying to simulate custom backoff trajectories offline using recorded gradient distributions, the lack of an isolated, pure-Python gradient scaler update loop makes it impossible to verify the exact history of scale factors without launching a full distributed training job. Furthermore, diagnosing why a specific gradient tensor distribution triggers a scale reduction requires inspecting the maximum absolute values or finite-check logic independently from C++ hooks.

The task is to build a complete, pure-Python GradScaler module and update loop that accurately computes scale trajectories, tracks growth intervals, detects inf/NaN states, and derives the largest safe loss scale from a given gradient distribution. This will enable offline simulation of mixed-precision dynamics, ensuring stable training trajectories and robust verification of scale adaptation rules before deployment.
